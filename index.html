Enterprise data challenges

Data maturity built into the foundation.

Across industries and government, teams face common challenges in finding, connecting, protecting, and using information. Agentic AI solutions are often limited or stall out quickly. Why? The data isn't AI-ready. Semantic tools and practices can improve maturity in each area.

DiscoveryMake information easier to find and understand.Explore the challenge

The challenge: Teams cannot use data they cannot locate or interpret.

An approach: Describe assets with meaningful metadata, identifiers, and shared definitions that support search and understanding.

Related principle: FAIR findability.

Access & securityLeverage edge compute, cybersecurity posture, and access controls.Explore the challenge

The challenge: People need useful access while sensitive information remains protected.

An approach: Describe data, sensitivity, and usage requirements clearly to inform access and governance decisions alongside security controls.

Related principles: FAIR accessibility and NIST privacy and cybersecurity frameworks.

InteroperabilityMake meaningful connections between data across systems and domains.Explore the challenge

The challenge: Different systems use different structures, terms, and assumptions.

An approach: Use shared vocabularies, models, and open standards to align meaning and support exchange.

Related principle: FAIR interoperability.

Quality & reuseMake data more dependable and useful over time.Explore the challenge

The challenge: Missing context, unclear provenance, and inconsistent quality make reuse difficult.

An approach: Document origins and usage conditions, define quality expectations, and support review and validation.

Related principle: FAIR reusability; NIST RDaF metadata and provenance.

Meaning & inferenceExpress relationships so knowledge can be put to work.Explore the challenge

The challenge: Implicit relationships and assumptions are hard for people and systems to use consistently.

An approach: Represent concepts and relationships using linked-data standards such as RDF, RDFS, and OWL; where appropriate, use inference to derive additional knowledge.

Related principle: FAIR interoperability and knowledge representation.

Enterprise data maturity may be measured and governed by public standards, such as the FAIR principles, the NIST Research Data Framework, and the DHS Evidence-Based Data Strategy. Controlled vocabularies, taxonomies, ontologies, shared semantic data layers, and knowledge graphs are technologies that can provide relief for many enterprise pain-points.
